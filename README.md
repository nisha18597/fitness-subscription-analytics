Fitness Subscription Analysis — Key Insights

Overview

This analysis uses the Fitness Subscriptions Dataset to answer three business questions:

After how many months do customers typically cancel their subscriptions?

What subscription revenue is expected over the next 12 months, and is there evidence of seasonality?

How long do the 2024 and 2025 customer cohorts take to recover their Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)?

1. Customer Cohort Analysis

Customers were grouped by the month of their first subscription transaction. Customer tenure was measured as the number of months between the customer's first and last observed subscription transaction.

Key insight

Customers typically cancel relatively early in their subscription lifecycle.

Overall median observed subscription tenure: approximately 2 months

2024 cohort median: approximately 3 months

2025 cohort median: approximately 2 months

This indicates that the largest retention challenge occurs during the first few months after acquisition. The cohort matrix in Tableau can be used to visualize how retention declines as customers move further from their subscription start month.

Interpretation: Customers typically cancel after about 2–3 months, with the 2025 cohort showing a shorter observed tenure than the 2024 cohort.

Note: Cancellation is inferred from the customer's last observed subscription transaction in the available dataset. Because the dataset ends in January 2026, newer cohorts have less time to be observed and their tenure can be understated.

Seasonality

The forecast shows a modest seasonal pattern, with revenue increasing toward the end of the year and reaching its highest forecast level in December, followed by a decline in January.

However, the historical dataset contains only about two years of monthly observations, and revenue has a strong overall upward trend. Therefore, the evidence for seasonality should be interpreted cautiously. The dominant pattern in the data is continued revenue growth, with some indication of recurring monthly variation.

Interpretation: Subscription revenue is expected to continue increasing, reaching approximately $255.8K in December 2026, with total forecast revenue of approximately $2.64M over the following 12 months.

3. CAC vs. LTV

To evaluate CAC payback, cumulative revenue generated by each customer cohort was compared with the average CAC for that cohort.

For this analysis, LTV was based on total customer transaction revenue, including subscription revenue and other recorded purchases such as fitness challenges, nutrition plans, personal training, and annual upgrades.

Interpretation

The 2025 cohort took longer to recover its acquisition cost than the 2024 cohort. This indicates that the newer cohort generated revenue more slowly during the first several months of its customer lifecycle.

Because the 2025 cohort has a shorter observation window in the dataset, its longer payback period should be interpreted with caution.

Overall Business Insights

Customers tend to cancel relatively early, with a typical observed tenure of about 2–3 months.

Subscription revenue shows a strong upward trend and is forecast to generate approximately $2.64M over the next 12 months.

The forecast shows some seasonal variation, particularly stronger revenue toward the end of the year, but the historical period is too short to treat seasonality as a definitive pattern.

The 2024 cohort recovered CAC in about 4 months, while the 2025 cohort recovered CAC in about 6 months based on cumulative total revenue.

Early-month customer retention is an important area for improvement because faster churn can delay CAC recovery and reduce customer lifetime value.

Data and Methodology Notes

Cohorts are based on each customer's first observed subscription transaction.

Subscription cancellation/tenure is inferred from the last observed subscription transaction.

Revenue forecasting uses monthly subscription revenue and a Holt-Winters model with trend and seasonal components.

CAC payback compares cumulative customer transaction revenue with average cohort CAC.

The dataset covers transactions from January 2024 through January 2026.
